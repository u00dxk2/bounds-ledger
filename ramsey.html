Small Ramsey numbers R(k, l)

What the survey Small Ramsey Numbers prints for every two-colour classical Ramsey number R(k, l) with 3 ≤ k ≤ 10 and k ≤ l ≤ 15, exactly as printed, with the reference it credits for each value, and what this ledger has and has not checked about it.

The source. Stanisław Radziszowski, Small Ramsey Numbers, The Electronic Journal of Combinatorics, dynamic survey DS1, revision #18, 24 April 2026, Section 2.1, Tables Ia and Ib. The survey is the authority. This page reproduces its values and reference keys, not the document.

What is covered. Section 2.1 only: 72 values of R(k, l). Table Ia prints something for 72 of them, and Table Ib prints a newer upper bound for 50. Not covered: everything else in the survey, including other graphs, more than two colours and hypergraphs, and any result published after revision #18.

What is watched. Once a day a scheduled job checks the journal’s page and the author’s revision list for a newer revision, and re-reads this table from the survey’s PDF, whose fingerprint is pinned. A new revision turns that job red until a person has compared it with this page. The run history is the live read; this page is not.

What has been checked against its source. The survey credits each bound to a paper by the key shown beside it, such as [AnM4]. Opening each paper and reading it against the entry is the next step, and until an entry has been read, it says not yet read. So far: sound 0 (read, and it supports the entry) · defective 0 (read, and it does not support the entry) · unresolved 0 (reached, and could not settle it) · unreachable 0 (could not be read) · not yet read 72.

Table Ia and Table Ib can print different upper bounds for the same number. Table Ib lists upper bounds from computations by Angeltveit and McKay ([AnM2], [AnM3], [AnM4]) and R(4, 5) from [MR4], and the survey says they improve on the upper bounds in Table Ia. Where both tables print one, this page shows both and does not choose between them for you.

[HW+] is the survey’s own abbreviation of [HWSYZH], as enhanced in [Boza5]. A reference written as item 2.3.h of the survey points to a numbered item of its Section 2.3, not to a paper.

If an entry disagrees with the survey or with the paper it credits, use looks wrong? on that entry. The report arrives already naming the entry and the revision.
