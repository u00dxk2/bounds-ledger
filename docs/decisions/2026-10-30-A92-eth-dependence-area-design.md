## The idea

A theorem proved under ETH stays true as a conditional statement. But its hypothesis is now reported refuted, so a reader citing the theorem as evidence that a problem is hard needs to know that the support has been reported withdrawn. The ledger would record, per paper, what the paper itself states its result rests on. It never says the result is false.

## What the ledger would assert, per paper

The minimum, and nothing stronger: **this paper states its result R under hypothesis H, and H is reported refuted, by one of two named routes.** Route 1, *by implication*: H implies ETH, so ETH's failure refutes it (SETH, Gap-ETH, randomized ETH). Route 2, *directly*: the reported algorithms contradict H themselves, although ETH's failure alone would not. `#ETH` is route 2 only. ETH implies #ETH, not the reverse, but both preprints give subexponential *counting* algorithms, which contradict #ETH directly. Every row names its route. The two ETH preprints do not by themselves refute Orthogonal Vectors (the Anthropic paper says so), the fine-grained k-Clique conjecture or the Set Cover Conjecture: falsifying ETH gives no faster algorithm for any of them. **3SUM and APSP are not standing either: they are reported refuted by a separate paper** (read in cycle 3, 2026-10-30; see "3SUM and APSP, read" below). A row resting on them, or on Exact Triangle, Zero-Weight k-Clique or Min-Weight k-Clique, never counts as `alternate support survives`. The source of that sentence is the paper's own statement of R (a theorem number and page), never the abstract alone, and never a list. The ledger would not assert that R is false, that R fails without H, or that no other proof of R exists.

Verdicts. The pilot found two cases the draw rule's four verdicts did not separate, so the set below has six:

| Verdict | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `ETH-FAMILY` | A main result is stated only under ETH or a hypothesis refuted by route 1 or 2 above, and the paper gives no proof under a hypothesis left standing. |
| `ETH-STATED-PROVED-VIA-OTHER` | The result is stated under SETH (or another ETH-family hypothesis), but the paper's own proof is a reduction from a problem whose hardness hypothesis the reported refutation leaves standing (typically Orthogonal Vectors). The statement as printed has lost its support; the proof's content survives under the other hypothesis. *(Pilot gap 1.)* |
| `OPTIMALITY-ONLY` | The main result is unconditional (usually an algorithm), and only its optimality ("tight under ETH") rests on ETH. The algorithm stands; only the tightness claim loses its support. *(Pilot gap 2.)* |
| `OTHER-HYPOTHESIS` | Conditional only on hypotheses the ETH preprints leave standing (OV, fine-grained k-Clique, Set Cover), or also proved under one. A row resting on 3SUM, APSP, Exact Triangle, Zero-Weight or Min-Weight k-Clique keeps this verdict but carries the separate status "reported refuted by Alman–Vassilevska Williams 2026" (see "3SUM and APSP, read"), and never counts as alternate support. |
| `NOT-CONDITIONAL` | ETH is mentioned, but no main result rests on it. |
| `UNREAD` | Not read. Never a verdict about the paper. |

Each verdict also carries its **evidence depth**: `abstract` or `statement` (the theorem as printed, with its number and page). **Only a `statement`-depth verdict could ever render.** An abstract-depth verdict is a screening result.

## How a refutation that no human has confirmed is worded

"Refuted" alone over-claims. The ledger would say, with the date:

> ETH is **reported refuted**: two preprints posted in October 2026, one from OpenAI and one from Anthropic, each claim an algorithm that contradicts it, and each publishes a Lean proof. Anthropic's paper says its Lean development was written by AI models and that, at the time of writing, no person was known to have checked it. OpenAI's release says its manuscripts are model-produced and names a working group (Impagliazzo, Paturi, Williams) that will examine its proof; its README and history report no human check yet. *(This ledger has not read OpenAI's paper itself; the sentence about OpenAI rests on those two documents only.)*

The wording changes only on a published reading by a human, such as that working group's report, and then it quotes that reading. A watch row would hold the clearing condition and its read command. A second clause applies to `ETH-STATED-PROVED-VIA-OTHER`: the surviving hypothesis is named, so the page never implies the result is gone.

## Where it would live, and the guards it needs

A new page, `eth.html`, separate from the constants index. **Only the three affected verdicts render as rows** (`ETH-FAMILY`, `ETH-STATED-PROVED-VIA-OTHER`, `OPTIMALITY-ONLY`), each at `statement` depth, because only those carry the per-paper assertion above. `OTHER-HYPOTHESIS` and `NOT-CONDITIONAL` are counted in a denominator line ("of N papers read from the frame, …"), and `UNREAD` is counted separately beside it ("M could not be read"). None of them is shown as a row, since a row would assert nothing about ETH. It is not a mirror and there are no bound cells, so neither `ledger/openai-math-map.json` (A-78's store, keyed by watched constants) nor `ledger/newer-results.json` (A-82's store, which assumes an exact evaluator for one constant) fits: the unit here is a third-party paper and its stated hypothesis. A new store would be `ledger/eth-dependence.json`. Each entry would hold the arXiv id, the theorem number and page, a quotation that must contain the hypothesis's name, the verdict, the depth, and the reader. Every row would be re-derivable by opening that page.

The page would need its own guard, in the shape the second area's page already has (`render-ramsey.mjs --check`):
- the committed page is regenerated and compared, as `--check` does;
- nothing loads from another host;
- the words "false", "wrong" and "record" are banned in any rendered verdict;
- each quotation is present in its entry, and each names its hypothesis;
- only the three affected verdicts at `statement` depth render as rows; every other entry is counted in the denominator line and never rendered;
- three separate sums, each refused when it fails: the counts of the five read verdicts sum to the papers read; papers read plus `UNREAD` sum to the frame entries processed; and the rendered rows equal the `statement`-depth entries among the three affected verdicts;
- each row's "looks wrong?" link is titled so `scripts/report-rate.mjs` counts it as an arrival.

**And the public-surface rule:** a row asserts something about another person's paper on a public page. So every row's text goes through the outward gate (adversarial review, a cross-family claims pass, David's yes) before the page exists at all.

## 3SUM and APSP, read (2026-10-30, cycle 3)

Read from two primary sources, not from news coverage or the research reports:
- **The paper.** arXiv 2610.06783v1, *Truly Subquadratic 3SUM and Truly Subcubic APSP via Triangles in Sparse Lopsided Graphs*, by Josh Alman and Virginia Vassilevska Williams, posted 2026-10-05. Its abstract, read through the arXiv API on 2026-10-30: deterministic 3SUM "on n integers of polynomial size in O(n^{1.9992}) time" and APSP "on directed n-vertex graphs with polynomially bounded integer weights in O(n^{2.9995}) time. This refutes the 3SUM and APSP hypotheses." It adds that, "using known reductions", it also refutes the real-valued versions of both, the Exact Triangle hypothesis, the Zero-Weight k-Clique hypotheses and three hinted Online Matrix–Vector conjectures. The authors are people; this ledger knows of no published referee report.
- **The Lean statement.** `3sum-apsp/EndStatement.lean` in `anthropics/formal-math` at `16a5ce4` (an upstream sha; it does not exist in this repo). It imports nothing. `Problem.SolvedInTime` reads: for every κ, one deterministic program, a slope b and a time bound T(n) = O(n^r), such that on every instance whose input integers have absolute value at most n^κ, and at every word size of at least b(⌊log₂ n⌋ + 1) bits, the program halts within T(n) steps with the right answer. `Theorem_22_3SUM` sets r = 1.9992; `Theorem_22_APSP` sets r = 2.99942 for directed graphs with no negative cycle (the printed Theorem 2 has 2.9995; Theorem 22's second algorithm gives the smaller one). The repository's `docs/MACHINE.md` says the machine is weaker than the standard word RAM (no division, shift or bitwise operation) and generous on four points that "change no exponent" (free zeroed memory, for one).

**The handoff's two questions, answered.** The range of ν: the statement quantifies over EVERY κ, so it covers every polynomial bound on the integers. The weight range: polynomially bounded integers. The usual forms, as this ledger knows them (from memory of the literature, not from a source read today): integer 3SUM needs n^{2−o(1)} time on a word RAM with O(log n)-bit words, for integers in a polynomial range such as {−n^3, …, n^3}; APSP needs n^{3−o(1)} for integer weights of polynomial size and no negative cycle. Both ranges are inside what the Lean statement covers. So, if these statements hold, they contradict both hypotheses as usually stated, which is also what the paper's abstract says. A deterministic algorithm is also a randomized one, so the randomized forms fall too.

**What is Lean-stated and what is not.** Stated in Lean, with proofs per the repository: integer 3SUM, the (min,+)-product, APSP, Exact Triangle (Theorem 19), Zero-Weight k-Clique for every k ≥ 3 (Corollary 39), and, in `PaperStatements.lean`, Min-Weight and Max-Weight k-Clique for every k ≥ 3 at the same exponent (`Corollary_39_min_max`). NOT proved as a running time: the real-valued versions. The repository's README says the running times on real inputs are "not proved for any machine". The supporting mathematics for real inputs is Lean-stated, and the final deductions exist only as conditional lemmas of its library. So for the real-valued versions the ledger's "reported refuted" rests on the paper's abstract. This ledger has not compiled the proofs, has not run Comparator, and has not read the paper past its abstract.

**What this changes for A-92.**
1. The wording follows ETH's: **reported refuted**, with the date and the sources, never "refuted". It changes only on a published reading by a person.
2. A theorem resting on integer 3SUM, APSP, Exact Triangle, Zero-Weight k-Clique or Min-Weight k-Clique has no alternate support that survives. Its verdict stays `OTHER-HYPOTHESIS`, because the area is ETH and these hypotheses are not ETH-family. Its row carries a second status, "reported refuted by Alman–Vassilevska Williams 2026", and it is never counted in "alternate support survives". A theorem resting only on the real-valued versions carries the same status, labelled as resting on the paper's abstract.
3. Refinement 6(a) above stands: the *unweighted* fine-grained k-Clique conjecture (n^{ωk/3−o(1)}) is not touched by this paper's abstract or its Lean statements. Orthogonal Vectors and Set Cover are not touched either.
4. Whether results resting on 3SUM or APSP become a watched area of their own is David's call. This design does not propose it.
